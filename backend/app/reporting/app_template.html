What HOLD and SELL mean here

HOLD means none of the strategy's four exit rules has been met. SELL means one has. Neither is advice, and neither is a forecast — they are what a set of rules says about a price series.

The rules were chosen before you bought anything, which is the point of having them: the decision gets made when you are not holding the position.

The four ways a position ends

The target is not a prediction. It does not mark where the price is expected to turn. It is three times what you risked, fixed in advance so taking a profit is decided before there is a profit to feel anything about. Four times out of five the price never reached it.

Notice which row is largest. The trend break ends more positions than the stop and the target together, and it is the one without a fixed level — it moves.

None of this establishes that the strategy works. Two of every three backtest trades lost money. What carried the sample was a small number of winners held for more than a month — which a few trades a year cannot reproduce. Every figure describes one historical sample, and a pattern repeating is an assumption, not a finding.
Your positions never leave this phone. They are in this browser's storage. This page is served from a public repository, so anything it carried would be public — it carries prices, and the arithmetic about your money happens here.
No part of this system places orders, and live trading is not implemented. Prices come from free providers with no service guarantee and are previous closes, not live quotes. Every instrument is US-listed and priced in USD — the Chilean and Asian ones are ADRs and country ETFs, so a return carries the currency move as well.
